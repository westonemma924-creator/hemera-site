Weekly Report Prompt Pack

First, a short frame for what to report and why. Then the all-in-one prompt from film 01 and five single-purpose prompts that turn raw weekly numbers into a first-draft report. Copy one, paste your data in place of [paste], and edit the result.

Start here: why report at all

A weekly report is not paperwork. It is how you notice a problem on Tuesday instead of finding it on Friday. If you run a small company, you do finance, projects, people and sometimes marketing yourself, so the report is the one place where all four hats meet.

A good report answers three questions, in this order:

  • What changed? Compared with last week, not with a perfect world.
  • Why do I think so? A hypothesis is fine. Mark it as one.
  • What do I do about it? One action, one owner, one date.

What to track: one metric, one decision

Track a number only if you know which decision it would change. If it changes nothing, drop it. Five to seven numbers are enough. These are examples, not benchmarks. Pick what fits your business.

  • Finance ops: cash in and out this week, overdue invoices (count and value), budget versus actual for your biggest cost line.
  • Projects: milestones on track versus late, open blockers, scope changes accepted this week.
  • People: open roles, overtime or absence in total (never by named person), handovers planned.
  • Marketing: new leads, how many replied, spend versus plan.

For each number write down three things once: where it comes from, who owns it, and at what size of change you want to be told. That is your reporting frame. It takes about 15 minutes and makes every later report faster.

Where AI helps, and where it does not

AI is good at the slow part: comparing two tables, drafting the summary, rewriting it for another reader. It does not know your business, so you supply the data, the thresholds and the decisions. You check every number against its source before the report goes anywhere.

Before you start

Data checklist: before you paste anything

Do this once per report, then keep the list next to your template.

  • Replace names of people with roles or codes (Person A, Team lead).
  • Replace client and supplier names with codes. Keep the key in a separate file, not in the prompt.
  • Remove emails, phone numbers, ID and account numbers, and contract values you are not allowed to share.
  • Never paste pay, health or absence data linked to a named person.
  • Read free-text notes: names hide in comments and email threads.
  • Check your organisation's AI policy and the tool's data settings before you start.
  • Remove names, client data and anything confidential before you paste a table into an AI tool. Check your organisation's policy first.
  • The AI writes a first draft. You read it and fix it. Never send anything you have not read.
  • Test on one report and measure the time before and after.
